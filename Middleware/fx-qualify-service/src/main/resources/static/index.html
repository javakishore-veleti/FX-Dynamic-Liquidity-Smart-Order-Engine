1. Overview

Service port 8082 · dedicated database fx_qualify_db

After quote, before execution: this service answers “May we trade this size/pair/tenor with which liquidity providers?” Failures become knock-outs; survivors become eligible LPs for route-exec.

2. Plain-language glossary

Order (what it looks like here)

An FX order is a client request to buy or sell a currency pair for a size and tenor — not yet a completed trade. In qualify, the request typically carries fields like: clientOrderId, clientId, ccyPair (e.g. EURUSD), tenor (SPOT / TOM / 1M), notionalUsd, and side when provided upstream. After qualify succeeds, route-exec turns survivors into a parent execution order plus child LP splits.

Everyday picture: “Client CORP_88 wants to BUY about $5M of EURUSD spot under order id CO-123.” That packet is the order; this service decides if/where it is allowed to go.

Qualification / order qualification

Qualification means running pass/fail checks before sending the order to LPs for execution. Order qualification is that whole gate for one order: settlement policy + credit headroom → either qualified=true with eligible LPs (and a credit reservation token), or qualified=false with knock-out reasons. API: POST /api/v1/fx/orders/qualify.

How it differs from nearby steps:

  • Quote — “What price might we show?” (no commitment to trade)
  • Qualify — “Are we allowed to trade this, and with whom?”
  • Route-exec — “Send/fill and record the trade with those LPs”
Settlement

The actual exchange of currencies after a trade is agreed — “you give me euros, I give you dollars” on the value date. Pricing can happen now; settlement is when money really moves (spot is often about T+2).

Cutoff (settlement cutoff)

A clock deadline after which you should not treat the deal as eligible for that settlement path today. Stored as cutoff_time_utc on the policy row. If the pair/tenor is CLS-eligible and current UTC time is after the cutoff → knock-out PAST_CLS_CUTOFF.

Like “last boarding time.” Miss it and that flight (today’s CLS settlement window) is closed; seeded example: EURUSD SPOT cutoff 23:55:00 UTC vs EURUSD TOM 16:00:00 UTC.

Settlement eligibility / “against settlement cutoffs”

Whether this order is allowed to go to settlement under bank policy for that pair and tenor. “Order qualification against settlement cutoffs” means: look up the policy for the order’s pair+tenor, then fail if past cutoff (when CLS applies), outside min/max clip, restricted, or unsupported.

Differs per row: EURUSD SPOT vs EURUSD 1M can have different cutoffs, clip sizes, and CLS flags. Same client, different tenor → different pass/fail.

Credit

A bilateral risk limit: how much FX exposure the bank will allow for a client trading with a given LP (liquidity provider), measured in USD notional. Like a credit-card limit, but between institutions for FX. Stored in the in-memory/Redis credit store as maxCreditUsd per clientId + lpId.

Utilization

How much of that limit is already used up. Here: utilizedUsd (already on the books) plus reservedUsd (held for in-flight qualifies). Headroom = limit − utilized − reserved (room left to take a new order).

Credit utilization

The combined picture: limit vs used vs reserved vs headroom for a client/LP pair. API: GET /api/v1/fx/credit/utilization/{clientId}. During qualify, if reserving the new notional would exceed headroom → knock-out INSUFFICIENT_CREDIT for that LP. Warnings fire around 85% / hard signals around 95% utilization.

Limit $25M, used $2.5M, reserved $0 → headroom $22.5M. A $30M order fails credit for that LP even if settlement policy passes.

Venue (in this repo)

A place you can send the FX order to be filled — here, a liquidity provider (LP) such as LP_CITI, LP_JPM, LP_GS. “Venue” ≈ “which bank/market-maker counterparty.” It is not the Angular portal; portals are UIs, venues are trading destinations.

Knock-out

A hard “no” that removes a venue from the eligible list for this order. The LP is knocked out of contention; route-exec should not send size there (for that attempt). Examples from code: PAIR_TENOR_UNSUPPORTED, RESTRICTED_CCY, BELOW_MIN_CLIP, ABOVE_MAX_CLIP, PAST_CLS_CUTOFF, INSUFFICIENT_CREDIT.

Sports picture: a team is disqualified from the matchup. Other teams (LPs) may still play.

Venue knock-out rules

The checklist that decides pass/fail per LP: settlement policy rules (often apply to all LPs the same) plus per-LP credit headroom. See POST /api/v1/fx/venues/knockout-check and the knockouts list on qualify.

Route-exec (fx-route-exec-service :8083)

The next microservice after qualify. It routes the order to surviving LPs and executes (records parent order + child venue splits, failover, TCA trace). Qualify decides who is allowed; route-exec does the send/fill bookkeeping.

Airport security (qualify) then boarding/flight ops (route-exec). You only board airlines that passed security.

3. What gets validated (the three buckets)

BucketPlain meaningHow it differs
Settlement eligibility Is this pair/tenor/size/time allowed to settle? Varies by ccy_pair + tenor policy row (cutoff, clips, CLS, restricted)
Credit utilization Does this client still have limit room with that LP? Varies by clientId + lpId credit line
Venue knock-outs Which LPs are eliminated for this order? Policy fails often knock out all LPs; credit fails knock out one LP at a time

4. Business features

  • Order qualification: pass/fail gate before route-exec
  • Settlement cutoff / clip / CLS / restricted checks per pair+tenor
  • Credit utilization / headroom per client and LP
  • Venue knock-outs with reason codes; eligible LP list for survivors

5. Database tables

Database: fx_qualify_db

  • settlement_cutoffs_and_policies — config/master pricing-ops rules per pair+tenor (CLS flag, cutoff UTC, min/max clip, restricted). Credit lines live in the credit store (memory/Redis), not this table.
